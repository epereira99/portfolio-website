# Memory walk: a 2D Journey (design)

Date: 2026-11-02. This **replaces the 3D world** of the Journey described in `2026-11-01-portfolio-website-design.md` (§6–§10). Everything in that spec that isn't the 3D world still holds. Where they conflict, this document wins.

Tags: **[Eduardo]** = decided by Eduardo. [my call] = Claude's call, open to change.

Reference mockup: `mockups/memory-walk.html`. It is a throwaway spike: it shows the look and how scrolling feels, not the code structure.

## 1. Intent

The 3D Townscaper world didn't work: Eduardo couldn't recognise the landmarks and disliked how it looked. **[Eduardo]** The new idea is that the Journey is **a walk through Eduardo's memories**. A painted Eduardo walks away from the viewer across a navy, starry night. As he walks, painted memories of the places in his life drift out of the dark on the right, and the story of each stage appears on the left. **[Eduardo]** It should feel quiet, personal and nostalgic, like a dream, not a game.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**
- The visitor feels they are walking *with* Eduardo through his memories, forward and back, controlled by scroll. **[Eduardo]**
- Each landmark is recognisable at a glance (the main failure of the 3D version).
- Same story, content and navigation as today; only the world behind it changes. **[Eduardo]**

**Non-goals**
- No 3D, no WebGL. **[Eduardo]**
- Not deleting the 3D code. It stays in the repo, unused. **[Eduardo]**
- Not rewriting the intro, chapter cards, project overlays, project dialog, voyage map, ending, stage header or bottom bar. **[Eduardo]**

**Anti-goals**
- The landmarks looking like **framed paintings hung on a wall**. They are painted scenes whose edges dissolve into the night, like a memory surfacing. **[Eduardo]**
- A cartoon or flat-vector character. The look is painterly. **[Eduardo]**
- A small character. He is big: about the full height of the screen. **[Eduardo]**
- A character that only bobs. He really walks: legs and arms move. **[Eduardo]**

## 3. What stays from the current site [Eduardo]

Unchanged, reused as-is: `ScrollDriver` and the scroll→screens progress store; the timeline (`SEGMENTS`, `overlayAt`, length budget); `JourneyOverlay` (intro, chapter cards, project overlays, ending); `StageHeader`; `BottomBar` with the project fan; `ProjectDialog`; `VoyageMap`; all content in `src/content/`.

The 3D layer (`JourneyCanvas` and everything under `src/journey/scene/` and `src/journey/art/`) is **no longer rendered** on `/` but **stays in the repo**. **[Eduardo]** How it is parked (simply no longer imported, or behind a flag) is the builder's choice; it must not be loaded by the homepage bundle. [my call]

## 4. The scene

Layers, back to front:

1. **Sky.** A deep navy night gradient (lighter toward the bottom centre). **[Eduardo]** for navy and stars.
2. **Stars streaming toward the viewer.** Their depth is tied to scroll progress, so scrolling down they rush past (walking forward) and scrolling up they recede (walking back). This is the main cue of forward motion. [my call]
3. **Memories** (§6), on the right. **[Eduardo]**
4. **Eduardo** (§5), centred, in front.
5. **The existing 2D UI** on top (text on the left, header, bottom bar, dialog).

Text over the scene stays readable. The night palette makes this easy, but contrast is still checked. (Original spec §10.)

## 5. The character [Eduardo]

- **Look:** a painterly illustration of Eduardo seen from behind (dark navy jacket, jeans, dark sneakers), based on his photo and generated by Eduardo with Nano Banana, then animated with an image-to-video tool.
- **Source:** `gemini_generated_video_fd5727de.mp4` (720×1280, 24 fps, 10 s, flat #00FF00 background, walking in place). It goes into the repo as the source asset; the builder picks the folder.
- **Loop:** frames **61 to 199** (139 frames at 24 fps). Frame 200 nearly matches frame 61, so this loops seamlessly. Found by comparing frame differences in the spike. [my call]
- **Cut-out:** green removed with a soft edge and green spill suppressed. A faint light-blue fringe remains and reads as moonlight rim light on navy; acceptable. [my call]
- **Size and position:** centred, about 97% of the viewport height, feet just at the bottom edge. **[Eduardo]** for "big"; the exact numbers are [my call].
- **Scroll-driven walk:** the frame shown is picked from the distance walked (scroll progress), never from time. Scroll down and the walk plays forwards; scroll up and it plays backwards; stop and he freezes mid-step. **[Eduardo]** Progress is eased (smoothly follows the scroll) so the walk doesn't stutter. In the spike, 150 frames per stage unit felt right as a starting value. [my call]
- **Weight budget:** the spike's 139 full PNG frames are ~27 MB, far too heavy. Target **≤ 4 MB** for the whole walk (e.g. WebP or AVIF frames at a sensible height, or a sprite atlas), loaded after the intro text is usable. The method is the builder's call. Frames must be fully loaded before scrubbing starts. Until then he shows as a single still frame. [my call]
- **Ending:** he stops walking at the Stockholm ending. The original spec had him turn to face the viewer; in 2D that needs a front-facing painted image, which Eduardo may generate later. Until then he simply stands still. [my call]

## 6. The memories

Painted scenes of real places that fade in, drift past and fade out as Eduardo walks by. **[Eduardo]**

**Behaviour** [my call, as in the spike]: each memory has a position along the path. While still ahead it is faint and small near the centre; as he approaches it grows, slides out to the right and reaches full opacity alongside him; once passed it fades away. All of this is tied to scroll, so it reverses on scroll up. Only one or two memories are visible at once.

**Edges [Eduardo]:** no frame, no border. The edges dissolve into the night with a soft radial mask, done in code (not baked into the images) so the fade is consistent and tunable. [my call] for the code mask.

**The set** (landmarks as already agreed in the original spec **[Eduardo]**, plus the ship [my call]):

| Stage | Memories |
|---|---|
| Braga | Arcada, Bom Jesus do Monte, Universidade do Minho |
| Porto | Dom Luís I bridge, Clérigos tower |
| Voyage | the 1500s Portuguese nau (§7) |
| Jönköping | Jönköping University |
| Stockholm | Stadshuset |

Spacing within each stage's screen budget is the builder's call; memories of a stage shouldn't overlap so much that neither is readable.

**Images:** generated by Eduardo. **[Eduardo]** Brief for each: the same fixed style line in every prompt (painterly gouache, soft visible brush strokes, muted nostalgic palette, Ghibli/Shinkai-inspired, light grain); subject centred with the outer 15–20% unimportant (it gets faded); no borders; night or dusk lighting preferred; portrait ~4:5; ≥ 2000 px on the long side; PNG. [my call] for the brief.

**Weight:** each memory is compressed to WebP or AVIF at display size, **≤ 300 KB each**, by the same `tools/` script as the walk frames, and covered by the weight test. Memories load per stage, ahead of when they're needed. [my call]

**Until the images exist:** a soft placeholder per memory (e.g. a blurred colour wash with the landmark name), so the site works end to end. The public launch needs the real images (original spec §13). [my call]

## 7. The voyage [my call]

The original voyage (walk to the river, board the nau, map, landing) is kept in spirit but without 3D:

1. At the end of Porto, the **nau memory** rises up ahead, larger than the others and centred on the path, not on the right.
2. As he walks into it, the scene fades into the existing **`VoyageMap`** (parchment map, dotted route, small ship), which plays exactly as today. Eduardo is hidden during the map.
3. When the map ends, it fades back to the night sky with Eduardo walking, and the first Jönköping memory appears.
4. The Jönköping welcome banner (original spec §6 and §11.1; 3D `WelcomeBanner` today) becomes a line of text fading in on arrival, in the overlay's style.

## 8. Phones [my call]

No room for left and right: Eduardo stays centred but smaller (about 60% of the height), memories appear above him, and text below. Same scroll behaviour. Use smaller walk frames on small screens if that helps the weight budget.

## 9. Reduced motion and fallbacks [my call]

- `prefers-reduced-motion`: stars don't stream (static starfield), Eduardo shows as one still frame, memories and text cross-fade without sliding or scaling.
- No WebGL fallback is needed any more (nothing uses WebGL). The static vertical timeline from the original spec §10 was never built and is **dropped**: all content is ordinary DOM reached by scrolling, and the reduced-motion mode above covers visitors who turn animation off. [my call]
- Nothing is reachable only through the animation (original spec §10 still applies).

## 10. Technology [my call]

- Plain DOM, CSS and a 2D `<canvas>` for the stars, inside the existing Next.js app. No new dependencies are needed. The spike did all of it with a few dozen lines.
- One `requestAnimationFrame` loop reads the existing progress store (`getScreens()`), eases it, and updates the stars, the memories and the walk frame. No React re-render per frame.
- Asset preparation (keying the video, cutting the loop, compressing the frames) is a repeatable script in `tools/`, matching how `tools/blender/` works today, so a better video can replace the current one by re-running it. "No new dependencies" means runtime dependencies: the script may use a system tool such as ffmpeg (installed with Homebrew), like the Blender scripts rely on Blender. The source video (currently `~/Downloads/gemini_generated_video_fd5727de.mp4`) is copied into the repo first.
- The spike's "150 frames per stage" becomes frames per screen of scroll on the site, because stages are 1.5–2 screens long.
- The voyage's nau moment fits in the start of the voyage segment, before the map appears (today `phase.ts` holds the map back for the first 0.2 of that segment). The welcome banner fits in the delay Jönköping's chapter card already has (`chapterDelay`).
- Tests in the existing Vitest setup for the pure parts: frame index from progress (including negative wrap when walking back), memory visibility/position from distance, and the asset weight budget (like the current `assets.test.ts`).
